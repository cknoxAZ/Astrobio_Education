Active and inquiry-based learning

Active and inquiry-based learning are research-informed best practices that engage students in developing understanding through purposeful intellectual work. In astrobiology, learners can ask questions, investigate phenomena, analyze data, build models, compare explanations, and revise claims in response to evidence. Active learning includes discussion, writing, and interpretation as well as hands-on activities; inquiry gives that work a question and a reason for gathering evidence. Effective implementation combines meaningful problems with clear learning goals, appropriate guidance, feedback, and reflection. Students do not need to discover everything independently: structured support helps them connect their investigations to scientific ideas and recognize what their evidence can—and cannot—establish.

Active learning

Invite students to observe, model, deliberate, create, and revise. Make their reasoning visible through field notes, evidence maps, peer discussion, and explanations that change with new information.

Research behind the approach

Freeman and colleagues’ meta-analysis of undergraduate STEM courses found improved student performance with active learning compared with traditional lecturing. For K–12 education, the National Research Council’s framework emphasizes learning scientific ideas through practices such as asking questions, analyzing data, constructing explanations, and arguing from evidence.

  • Freeman et al. (2014), Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics
  • National Research Council (2012), A Framework for K–12 Science Education: scientific and engineering practices
