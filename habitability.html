Question 02 / Look beyond the habitable-zone label

Where could life exist?

A habitable environment could support life under specified conditions. Habitability depends on interacting factors, including accessible energy, chemistry, liquid water for life as we know it, and how conditions change over time.

Reviewed October 17, 2026 · Secondary & introductory undergraduate

Start here.

A planet’s distance from its star is only part of the story. Atmospheres, geology, ice, and internal heating can shape environments. An icy moon could contain liquid water below its surface even when its exterior is extremely cold.

Mission connection

Europa Clipper

Launched in 2024; traveling toward Jupiter, with arrival planned for 2030.

The mission investigates Europa’s potential habitability. It is not designed to deliver a definitive detection of life.

Bring it into the classroom

Build a habitability argument

Teams receive a world’s environmental profile. Rank the evidence for water, energy, and useful chemistry. Recommend one additional measurement and explain why it matters.

Evidence of learningAsk students to submit a supported claim, one alternative explanation or perspective, and a question that remains unresolved.

Suggested format: 20–30 minutes in pairs or small groups, followed by a brief written reflection. Adapt materials and complexity for your learners.

Begin with place

Compare shaded and exposed ground without collecting material. Use observations to ask how habitat conditions vary over very short distances.

Humanities & ethics connection

Habitable for whom? Distinguish conditions for microbes from conditions suitable for humans, and consider how language shapes exploration priorities.

Connect the 5 Ps

Place: begin with a nearby microhabitat. Personal: invite learners’ own questions. Practice: justify a measurement choice.

Revisit the full framework →

What we know

Evidence gives us a starting point.

Earth demonstrates that life can occupy many environments. Evidence supports an ocean beneath Europa’s ice, making its chemistry and energy sources important research targets.

What remains uncertain

Keep the question open.

Does Europa have the combination of conditions needed for life? How do materials move between its surface, ice shell, and interior?
