Humanities and ethics

Humanities and ethics are integral to understanding astrobiology as a human endeavor. Philosophy helps learners examine what counts as life and what evidence would justify a claim; history, literature, art, and cultural studies reveal how people have imagined other worlds and understood their place in the universe. Ethical inquiry asks what responsibilities accompany exploration, whose interests and knowledge inform decisions, and how we should treat unfamiliar forms of life or potentially inhabited environments. Bringing these perspectives into teaching helps students distinguish scientific evidence from value judgments, consider alternative interpretations, and develop reasoned positions on questions that scientific measurements alone cannot resolve.

A University of Arizona pathway

Applied Humanities — Astrobiology

The University of Arizona’s BA in Applied Humanities offers an Astrobiology emphasis that brings life and physical sciences together with humanities approaches to science and society. The program connects these areas with pathways in education, science journalism, research administration, and space policy.
