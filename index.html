01 / Ways into the subject

Astrobiology is a
human question, too.

Where did we come from? What makes a place livable? How would we recognize life? These questions invite more than one discipline—and more than one way of knowing. Start with a meaningful question, then choose the approaches that help students investigate it.

01

Place-based astrobiology

Use nearby soils, rocks, water, heat, and living things to explore habitability. Connect environmental observations with the histories and relationships that make a place meaningful.

Try thisCompare a shaded and exposed microhabitat. What evidence explains differences in conditions? Where does the comparison with Mars help—and where does it break down?
02

Interdisciplinary learning

Build a shared investigation that needs biology, chemistry, geology, astronomy, and human perspectives. Have students explain what each discipline contributes and what remains unresolved.

Try thisDesign a life-detection mission. Combine environmental measurements, biological evidence, communication, and an argument for responsible sampling.
03

Humanities & ethics

Examine how stories, language, philosophy, history, and cultural perspectives shape the search for life. Ask whose knowledge informs exploration and who participates in its decisions.

Try thisShould a potentially inhabited world be sampled? Separate scientific evidence from ethical commitments, compare perspectives, and recommend one exploration rule.
04

Active learning

Invite students to observe, model, deliberate, create, and revise. Make their reasoning visible through field notes, evidence maps, peer discussion, and explanations that change with new information.

Try thisPresent an ambiguous microscope sample. Students record observations, propose competing explanations, and identify the next test they would need.

02 / A connecting framework

Make it meaningful with the 5 Ps.

Use the LINCSS framework—Linking Inclusive Narratives to Create STEM Synergy—to connect astrobiology with communities, places, personal experience, scientific work, and possible futures.

01

People

Attend to the traditions, knowledges, and practices of cultural groups and communities, including people affected by scientific work.

Ask: Whose knowledge and experiences are included?

02

Place

Connect inquiry to local environments, histories, and relationships with land and water.

Ask: What can this place help us understand?

03

Personal

Invite learners’ interests, experiences, questions, and identities without requiring private disclosure.

Ask: What makes this question matter to me?

04

Practice

Make room for observing, modeling, interpreting evidence, collaboration, and revision.

Ask: How do we build and challenge an explanation?

05

Profession

Reveal varied roles and pathways, from laboratory research to art, education, policy, and communication.

Ask: Where could my strengths contribute?

Design note: Build relationships and seek permission before using community-held knowledge. Invite participation without asking any student to represent an entire community.
