Question 01 / From ingredients to living systems

How does life begin?

Life’s origins involve more than making organic molecules. Researchers investigate how chemistry could produce systems that maintain themselves, use energy, and pass on information with variation. Different hypotheses emphasize different environments and sequences of events.

Reviewed October 17, 2026 · Secondary & introductory undergraduate

Start here.

Organic molecules are carbon-containing compounds. Some are building blocks used by organisms, but they also form without life. Prebiotic chemistry studies processes that could precede biology. A complete account must connect molecules, environments, energy, and the emergence of evolution.

Ingredients → environment → processes → evidence

Research at the University of Arizona

Bennu in the laboratory

The UA-led OSIRIS-REx science team, led by Dante Lauretta, studies material returned from Bennu. University reporting describes evidence for organic building blocks and ancient brines.

Mission connection

OSIRIS-REx

Sample returned in September 2023; laboratory analysis continues.

Returned material allows researchers to study chemistry and minerals using instruments on Earth. It cannot by itself show how life originated.

Bring it into the classroom

Sort the evidence

Give groups three statement cards: organic molecules were detected; minerals indicate past water; life existed on Bennu. Ask which claims the findings support, and what evidence the remaining claim would require.

Evidence of learningAsk students to submit a supported claim, one alternative explanation or perspective, and a question that remains unresolved.

Suggested format: 20–30 minutes in pairs or small groups, followed by a brief written reflection. Adapt materials and complexity for your learners.

Begin with place

Compare how evaporation concentrates dissolved material in a local puddle or a supplied photograph. Explain why this is a model of one process, not a recreation of life’s origins.

Humanities & ethics connection

What assumptions enter a definition of life? Would a different definition change the experiments we choose?

Connect the 5 Ps

People: identify the many roles behind sample analysis. Practice: distinguish measurement from inference. Profession: connect chemistry, curation, and mission science.

Revisit the full framework →

What we know

Evidence gives us a starting point.

Bennu samples contain amino acids and nucleobases, alongside minerals that record interactions with water. These findings reveal ingredients and environmental history, not organisms.

What remains uncertain

Keep the question open.

Which settings and chemical pathways helped these ingredients become evolving living systems? How closely do laboratory experiments resemble early planetary environments?
