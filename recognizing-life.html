Question 03 / A signal needs an explanation

How would we recognize life?

A biosignature is a feature that may provide evidence of past or present life. Scientists evaluate it in context, test nonbiological explanations, and ask whether different lines of evidence agree.

Reviewed October 17, 2026 · Secondary & introductory undergraduate

Start here.

A moving particle, an organic molecule, or an unusual gas is not automatically a sign of life. Contamination, instrument effects, and physical or chemical processes can produce misleading signals. Good investigations make room for alternatives.

Observation ≠ interpretation ≠ conclusion

Research at the University of Arizona

Connecting laboratories and distant worlds

AABC’s research portfolio spans molecular and evolutionary biology, planetary environments, and exoplanet studies. These fields offer complementary ways to investigate life and interpret possible signals.

Mission connection

Perseverance

Operating on Mars; studying Jezero Crater’s rocks and environmental history.

The rover searches for signs of ancient microbial life and collects samples. A promising feature requires further testing and evaluation of nonbiological alternatives.

Bring it into the classroom

Make competing explanations visible

Show a microscope image or a coded water sample. Students write observations before interpretations, propose two explanations, and design a follow-up test. Revisit their confidence after discussion.

Evidence of learningAsk students to submit a supported claim, one alternative explanation or perspective, and a question that remains unresolved.

Suggested format: 20–30 minutes in pairs or small groups, followed by a brief written reflection. Adapt materials and complexity for your learners.

Begin with place

Examine a familiar sample or photograph before introducing a simulated extraterrestrial setting. The setting is fictional; the observation and reasoning are real.

Humanities & ethics connection

Who should announce an uncertain finding? Draft a headline that communicates interest without claiming a discovery of life.

Connect the 5 Ps

Practice: compare explanations. People: consider audiences affected by announcements. Profession: connect research, instrument design, and science journalism.

What we know

Evidence gives us a starting point.

Life on Earth leaves chemical, physical, and geological traces. Instruments can detect some of these features, but interpreting them requires knowledge of the surrounding environment.

What remains uncertain

Keep the question open.

Which combinations of observations would support a biological explanation strongly enough? How could we recognize life that differs from familiar Earth organisms?
