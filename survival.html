Question 04 / Survival, activity, and evolution

How does life survive and change?

Organisms respond to environmental challenges through physiology, behavior, and relationships. Across generations, populations evolve. Astrobiology uses Earth life to investigate possibilities and limits without assuming that another world hosts the same organisms.

Reviewed October 17, 2026 · Secondary & introductory undergraduate

Start here.

Surviving a temporary stress is different from growing and reproducing under that condition. Dormancy can reduce activity, but tolerance varies by species, exposure duration, and recovery conditions. Evolution concerns inherited changes in populations, not simply an individual coping with stress.

Individual response → population change → environmental history

Research at the University of Arizona

Biology belongs in astrobiology

AABC includes molecular and evolutionary biology alongside planetary research. Its education collection offers a route into exploring extreme environments and observing organisms.

Mission connection

Perseverance: environmental history

Operating on Mars.

Rover observations help reconstruct past environments and evaluate where evidence might be preserved. They do not demonstrate that Earth extremophiles could establish populations on Mars.

Bring it into the classroom

Survive—or thrive?

Give students hypothetical data showing recovery after stress and growth during stress. Ask them to distinguish the two outcomes and rewrite an exaggerated claim about an “indestructible” organism.

Evidence of learningAsk students to submit a supported claim, one alternative explanation or perspective, and a question that remains unresolved.

Suggested format: 20–30 minutes in pairs or small groups, followed by a brief written reflection. Adapt materials and complexity for your learners.

Begin with place

Observe organisms through existing images or prepared microscopy samples. Discuss changing moisture conditions in local habitats without deliberately stressing organisms.

Humanities & ethics connection

What responsibilities come with using living organisms in teaching and research? Design an observation activity that minimizes harm.

Connect the 5 Ps

Place: examine changing conditions. Practice: interpret data and limits. Personal: connect curiosity with care for living things.

Revisit the full framework →

What we know

Evidence gives us a starting point.

Earth organisms occupy a broad range of conditions. Studying them helps researchers identify mechanisms and boundaries relevant to planetary environments.

What remains uncertain

Keep the question open.

How do multiple stresses interact? Which limits reflect particular organisms, and which might be more general constraints on living systems?
